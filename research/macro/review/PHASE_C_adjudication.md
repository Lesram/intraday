## 1–2. Historical resemblance, look-ahead and effective sample

The independent blind specification uses expanding prior-only ranks, a minimum history, uniform publication-lag assumptions and eight starts separated by at least 60 months. Its six primary dimensions avoid forcing the stale CAPE splice into the answer. It selects 1964-07,1973-04,1988-11,1993-11,2000-03,2005-03,2011-07 and 2017-10. These are candidates under one explicit distance, not eight versions of the future.

Across these eight completed episodes, real total-market return medians are 4.57%,26.50%,24.63% at 1/3/5 years. The one-year range is−27.9% to+23.5%; the five-year range−24.12% to+129.84%. Removing the valuation dimension changes the five-year median to 48.09%, compared with 42.88% unconditionally. Across 4,955 random matched-size nonoverlapping sets, the lower-tail rank is 0.12109 and the descriptive two-sided tail fraction is 0.242. This is not strong evidence of a uniquely bad regime. See sealed [Phase B](PHASE_B_independent_regime_analysis.md), `phase_b/` and the post-seal [quantitative audit](phase_c/quantitative_adjudication.md).

The original “13” set is 12 greedy selections plus a manually added 2000 anchor. Multiple horizons overlap; a maximum 7 five-year starts can be separated without overlap and only 6 are mature. The forward-return routine clips a requested endpoint to the last available date. ANALOGS marks truncated outcomes with an asterisk, but the aggregation still includes November 2021 at 56 equity months and 57 gold/bond months in its five-year summary. Excluding that observation changes the original five-year medians: market 41.5%→27.0%(n 12), energy 56.7%→36.0%(n 12), gold 56.3%→25.2%(n 9). These are repairs to the original sample, not our primary specification.

Holding the prior feature set fixed gives an important partial replication:2007 and 2021 remain leading clusters under expanding ranks. Its threshold conjunction selects 5 months rather than 4, still concentrated in the 2000 and 2021–22 clusters. Therefore the headline resemblance is not wholly an artifact of full-sample ranking; exact ordering, feature availability, independent episode count and the inference drawn from it are the weaknesses. The original claimed expanding robustness list itself has no corresponding retained implementation and is not exactly reproducible.

The gold comparator also mixes eras: an 1871-onward baseline includes fixed-price/non-investable history excluded from parts of the selected sample. A floating-era or matched modern-era comparator materially reduces the claimed 45pp excess. Gold is a spot-price series; the bond comparison is a yield-based model, not a verified total-return index. These are further reasons to withhold allocation prescriptions.

Belief would require outcomes that survive prior-only ranking, a fixed distance/eligibility rule, complete horizons, nonoverlapping evaluation, reasonable feature deletion and period changes, consistent asset returns, and an untouched later sample. More overlapping monthly starts cannot substitute for more independent regimes. Current data are mostly revised; publication-lag approximations are only pseudo-out-of-sample, not vintage reconstruction.

## 3. CAPE: dates, earnings and the return denominator

The attachment itself misdiagnoses 41.3 versus 42.0. V2 documents 41.3 as a partial-September estimate from 12 closes through September 17 with extrapolated CPI;42.0 is August's monthly construction. The analog screen puts the September value in an August target vector. Align the date before comparing methodologies.

Both extend stale Shiller earnings using NIPA profits, which are not S&P 500 per-share earnings. Coverage, foreign earnings, accounting and buybacks differ. Matching a splice point does not solve those differences. Forward earnings and a genuine buyback-adjusted S&P history are not available in the supplied consistent series; they remain unavailable alternatives, not silently invented values. The quantitative appendix supplies explicitly hypothetical earnings/profit-share scenarios and identifies which change only the post-splice segment.

The high-CAPE return test also has stale return coverage: its code claims an extension but actually stops the Shiller return series inJune 2023. Original CAPE≥30 five-year results are n 68 monthly origins, median−12.53%,72.06% negative; the report's n 77 belongs to another horizon. Extending later returns with the explicitly different French broad-market series gives n 89, median−5.34%,55.06% negative. Using that broad-market universe consistently gives median+3.71%,49.44% negative for n 89. The substitution is not an exact S&P repair, but the sensitivity is enough to reject the precision of a mandatory negative-return claim.

The defensible conclusion is elevated valuation with material earnings and definition uncertainty. It does not supply a current position size. The new monitor exposes equity/GDP and profit share as context and withholds a fitted CAPE score.

## 4. Tightening into calm credit

The original 7/7 positive three-year real returns reproduce under its chosen tightening definition. That does not establish a causal or general zero-crash rule. A first-crossing definition of a 1 percentage-point increase in the three-month bill yield over 12 months, with prior-history 20th-percentile calm credit, selects 4 calm episodes including February 2000:−44.13% real three-year return and−44.99% path drawdown. Other threshold/spread/onset sensitivities are retained, not cherry-picked into a replacement rule.

Also distinguish a negative three-year endpoint from a crash during the path. A recovered endpoint can conceal a large drawdown. Calm spreads describe low current compensation for credit risk; they are neither a guarantee of resilience nor by themselves a trigger of collapse. A larger preregistered sample, independent onset definition and financing-spread history would be needed to promote this association to a forecast.

## 5. Profit share and mean reversion

The quantitative audit tests the question the first report left open. Estimated mean-reversion centres move roughly 6.1%→7.4%→10.3% across eras. A model trained before 2000 has five-year forecast RMSE 4.175 percentage points on the later holdout, versus 1.579 for persistence. This defeats a confident return to a fixed twentieth-century normal; it does not prove the present share is permanent.

Reported 12.07% is NIPA after-tax corporate profits/GDP for 2026Q2, not the earnings yield of the S&P. A useful scenario varies profit share and maps the effect transparently; it must not convert a historical average into a known equilibrium. Monitor revisions, definition changes and the denominator. Model uncertainty is the finding.

## 6. Concentration and what a proxy can establish

The French industry files contain firm counts and average market capitalization as well as returns. Multiplying count by average capitalization and normalizing yields an industry concentration proxy; returns alone are not weights. BusEq is 34.94% inMarch 2000 and 43.19% inJuly 2026, with a 45.27% peak inJune 2026. This supports unusually concentrated industry exposure in that universe.

It does not measure S&P top-ten weight, individual-company diversification or exact GICS technology concentration. At the declared high-concentration threshold, only 2 nonoverlapping completed five-year starts exist, one positive and one negative. The original two anecdotes cannot prove that concentration dictates which replacement sector to own. A useful next study requires consistent security/sector weights, delistings, actual rebalancing, fees and a predeclared comparator.

## 9 and 11. Prediction scoring and null forecasters

An asset, horizon and condition must be locked when a prediction is made. A scenario, a warning without a deadline, an ongoing fiscal trend and a point target are different contracts. Pending horizons are not misses; favourable prices cannot retrospectively turn a failed stated deadline into a hit. The blind transcript read, for example, leaves a September 2026 recession deadline unresolved as of September 19.

The new prediction reader registered rules and sealed 60 independent judgments before opening prior scored outcomes:32 hash-selected rows plus 28 separately labelled dated-endpoint candidates. The deliberately strict rules expose how much of the old scorecard comes from reviewer-supplied horizons. A separate 60-calendar-day researcher-defined experiment compares exactly matched asset/date windows; it is an operationalization sensitivity, not an accusation that an undated speaker made a 60-day forecast.

The raw register contains **288 predictions**; the prior scored file adds **five Jikh title-derived rows**, producing 293. This is a provenance distinction, not proof that five rows were fabricated. Its 54 HIT, 27 PARTIAL and 64 MISS labels create a scored denominator of 145: strict hits are 37.2%; awarding half credit to partials gives 46.6%. The 27 partials are 18.6% of scored calls, although only 9.2% of all 293 rows. These percentages depend on the scoring contract.

The sealed independent judgments were 35 UNSCORABLE, 15 PENDING, 8 CANNOT VERIFY and 2 MISS. Five explicitly documented **post-lock amendments**, based on dated Federal Reserve releases, produce the final audit: **35 UNSCORABLE, 11 PENDING, 7 CANNOT VERIFY, 4 HIT and 3 MISS**. The original lock remains intact. The primary 32-row probability sample contains 30 unscorable, one pending and one hit; the other 28 rows were deliberately selected for dated endpoints. Their counts must not be pooled into a population skill estimate.

Examples show why rescoring matters. Bravos's March 26 next-CPI prediction of 3.5–4% misses the subsequently observed March reading of about 3.26–3.29%; the old PARTIAL label relies on a generous tolerance. A relayed 4% Q 2 GDP estimate misses the supplied revised 1.48% annualized figure, although a first-release evaluation still requires the original vintage. A stated oil-inventory exhaustion deadline is not tested by substituting a later $150 Brent threshold. A one-to-three-year gold target remains pending. Conversely, a hike occurring before a stated year-end deadline can establish a hit immediately; the evaluator need not wait until December. Scoring that event does not verify a quoted market-implied probability.

The strict original-contract comparison has only **one matched scored case** after primary-source amendments: the June Fed hold, on which the forecast, no-change and persistence all agree. There is no informative skill estimate in that denominator. The broader old null table uses 113 mapped cases but mixes tolerances: an arbitrarily small increase can count as an upward forecast hit while also falling within a ±2% no-change band. Its headline percentages do not repair this mismatch.

A separate, clearly researcher-defined **60-calendar-day sensitivity experiment** retains 16 exact asset/date/direction cases from 69 original asset flags, with only 12 unique asset-date windows. It uses the same prices, maturity rule and ±2% outcome classification for every method:

| Method | Correct directions / 16 | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| Stated direction, operationalized at 60 days | 10 | 62.5% |
| Always up | 14 | 87.5% |
| Prior-60-day persistence | 11 | 68.75% |
| No change | 1 | 6.25% |

Bravos contributes 8/11 correct versus persistence's 7/11; Jikh contributes 2/5 versus persistence's 4/5. Thus even this sensitivity does **not** show both channels losing to persistence. Conditions are deliberately treated as hypothetical unconditional directions, and undated statements are not relabelled actual 60-day commitments. The small, selected, overlapping sample is neither a statistically established skill ranking nor a portfolio-return comparison. Its value is demonstrating how to pair a forecast and baseline without hiding exclusions.

Full selection accounting, original wording, conditions, amendments, primary-source links and benchmark definitions are in the [prediction rescoring report](phase_c/prediction_rescore.md), `prediction_rescore.csv`, `prediction_benchmark_selection.csv` and `prediction_benchmark_design.json`.

The old S&P “buy-and-hold” direction percentage cannot be a claim of portfolio underperformance: it includes different economic targets, gives no position weights, entry/exit implementation, financing, turnover or costs, and is not a matched multi-asset total-return strategy. The appropriate default remains a simple benchmark on the identical target and horizon. Skill must beat that baseline prospectively with enough independent calls.

## Additional load-bearing corrections

**Energy:** the original commodity-inflation≥75th-percentile sample has n 253 completed five-year monthly origins. Median paired Energy-minus-Market return is+10.70pp, mean+8.72pp, with 58.5% outperforming. Expanding-prior selection gives n 233, paired median+13.74pp and 61.37% outperforming;11 nonoverlapping starts give+26.08pp and 8/11 outperforming. The prior claim of disappearing five-year outperformance compares separate marginal medians, which is a different operator. These results do not prove a durable investable edge, but they reject the stated decay inference.

**Private performance:** lack of a public pre-April journal does not prove earlier trading was hypothetical. A five-year live record can coexist with a longer backtest; brief cash intervals do not contradict “primarily leveraged long.” The prior package makes several such unjustified inferences. The correct result is insufficient public evidence, without an accusation of invented trading. [Full V1/V3/V5/V8–9 package review](phase_c/package_review_events.md).

**Source verification:** the primary [Chicago Fed paper](https://www.chicagofed.org/publications/working-papers/2025/2025-09) places$849bn of private credit at 14% of life insurers' balance sheets in 2024. V6 treats 14% as market share and says Jikh's 42% tripled it. The verifier changed the denominator. A matched whole-market definition is needed to assess 42%; the original correction is false. [Root package notes](phase_c/package_review_root.md) also document oil-spread lower bounds treated as upper bounds, overlapping oil offsets, stock-versus-flow inference and substitute benchmarks that cannot establish the stated claim.

**As-of discipline:** later quarterly profits, August activity or September credit readings cannot settle what was known in March/May. Revised observations may settle a historical quantity but must be distinguished from information available at publication. A later intervention does not verify an earlier account of cumulative interventions.

**Inflation verdicts:** calendar-correct CPI is 2.391% inJanuary 2026,2.434% inFebruary,3.779% inApril and 4.167% inMay. V7's criticism that Bravos's 2.4% baseline “never printed” is itself wrong; those values closely match the corrected series. This is a substantive false accusation caused by the unpatched positional-year bug. It does not invalidate every inflation or real-wage claim; each formula must be checked.

**Model implementation:** `score()` omits the HY alternative, relative-claims deterioration branch and sustained-credit condition described in its prose. Three correlated level checks are not a calibrated risk model. Calling thresholds preregistered after inspecting the analog outcomes does not create an untouched test. Its “Sahm then equities rise” kill criterion targets the wrong outcome; “credit crosses while equities are high means it lags” reverses the possible timing relationship. A single positive return does not falsify a probabilistic long-horizon model. Treat model updates as versioned hypotheses with matching outcomes and complete false-alarm records.

**Real yields:** TIPS expected real yields and nominal yields minus realized historical inflation are different quantities. Restricting percentile history is good; the long-history proxy does not establish that the current expected real yield is ordinary. The static model also uses stale series/hard-coded context values. Store definition, vintage and window together.

## What atomisation missed, and what our approach misses

Phase A expected atomisation to miss narrative shape. Phase C confirms some but not all of that expectation. Brief 001 already noticed pitches, repeated opacity and several contradictions: it deserves credit for those. The blind pass adds systematic chronology, changing deadlines, source concentration, abandoned themes, conditional endings and the distinction between theme arrival and market lead. It does not prove source contagion or infer a sales calendar from urgency alone. The deterministic contradiction screen produces candidates, not automatically true contradictions.

Atomisation is the better tool for checking a specific date, law, unit, quotation or denominator. Its danger is scoring the true surrounding sentence instead of the decisive claim, weighting a long video's easy facts more heavily, and copying a theme-level verdict across dates. Narrative analysis preserves argument structure but invites its own subjective thematic interpretation and cannot replace accounting data or a matched forecasting experiment.

Next time: preserve full argument context; separate observation, attribution, mechanism, scenario, forecast and advice; preregister prediction contracts; mark compound claims explicitly; keep source acquisition and publication times distinct; sample videos prospectively; use independent blinded readers; record disagreements before resolution; and maintain an immutable forecast/outcome archive. Summarize exact counts and uncertainty, not a single authoritative-looking accuracy percentage.

## What Brief 001 got right that I would not have found

The blind corpus/regime pass would not by itself have found the detailed institutional trail for oil's physical-versus-futures dislocation, individual securitization letters, yen intervention arrangements, stablecoin reserve rules, or the specific denominator traps behind promotional market sizes. The original package is a useful source-discovery map even where its labels need rechecking. Distinguishing crude oil from total petroleum and transaction flows from holdings is particularly valuable; the review must apply those distinctions consistently to its own rebuttals.

Its attempt to build null forecasters, distinguish valuation from timing, publish falsifiers and log five self-corrections is methodologically better than merely matching scary analogs. The defects found here come partly from executing those good intentions inconsistently. We retain the intentions and replace the unsupported precision with explicitly bounded measurements.
