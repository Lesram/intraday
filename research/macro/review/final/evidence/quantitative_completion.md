# Final quantitative reconciliation

Prepared 20 September 2026. This supplement completes two previously missing comparisons and reproduces the disputed profit/CAPE arithmetic. It adds research evidence; it does not revise the sealed studies or establish an implementable strategy.

**The common-window comparison preserves a modest five-year valuation concern, but not a precise forecast. The joint rich-and-calm pool is materially sensitive to hindsight ranks and overlapping months. CAPE 50.30 is a historical denominator rewrite, not the predicted result of profits halving tomorrow.**

## 1. Design and reproducibility

Both screens are recomputed from their original feature definitions. This is more than restricting saved similarity scores to a common date window. The original full-history score is also recomputed as a diagnostic, with its saved CSV reproduced to its rounding precision. The independent review's reconstructed features and expanding distances reproduce its sealed outputs within 3×10⁻¹⁴ and 8×10⁻¹⁵ respectively.

The shared calendar is July 1964–July 2021: 685 monthly origins. Requiring all 60 subsequent returns in every included asset, plus exact CPI observations at origin and all one-, three- and five-year endpoints, leaves **684 eligible months**. October 2020 is excluded because its five-year CPI endpoint, October 2025, is missing. Outcomes are never truncated or interpolated. Eligibility depends on data availability, not the sign or size of a return.

The common outcome universe comprises **14 total-return series**: the French broad US market, Treasury bills and twelve value-weighted French industries. Returns run from the month after the origin through the exact endpoint, and real returns use the FRED CPI endpoint ratio. These are not S&P 500/GICS portfolios. No yield-modeled bond return, fixed-price historical gold or commodity price series is treated as a total-return asset. Gold and bonds are therefore outside this controlled comparison.

Each screen selects eight nearest origins with at least 60 calendar months between any pair. That removes overlap in these five-year return windows; it does not make economic cycles independent. The original model retains its 11-dimensional, equal-weight mean absolute distance with a seven-dimensional required backbone. Its expanding ranks use at least 120 strictly prior observations **per dimension**; the selected December 1967 case has ten available ranked dimensions. Its existing feature-date conventions and CAPE41.3/August target remain intact. The review model retains its six dimensions, joint history, equal-weight RMS distance and approximate publication lags with a September 18 target. Distances from the two models are not numerically comparable.

The current target is known today, and all historical input files contain revised data. “Expanding” excludes future observations from each historical ranking reference set; it does **not** supply a vintage-data out-of-sample trading test. The original model's current target percentile uses history before its target month. Its full-history diagnostic retains the original average-rank tie convention and original current-target convention. Preserving those conventions isolates the effect of replacing historical hindsight ranks rather than silently redesigning the models.

The pre-calculation choices are retained in [quantitative_design.json](quantitative_design.json). The executed script is [quantitative_completion.py](quantitative_completion.py); its [validation](quantitative_validation.json) is PASS and its [input hashes](quantitative_input_receipt.json) identify the consumed frozen files. From the repository root, reproduce with a Python environment containing pandas and numpy:

```sh
PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE=1 python3 research/macro/review/final/evidence/quantitative_completion.py
```

This writes only its generated quantitative/common/rich_calm/cape evidence files beside the script. It does not execute sealed generators. The authored explanatory report is not regenerated by that command. Local companion raw data and the two extracted French files are required; see the final package README for public-delivery limits. The [French data library](https://mba.tuck.dartmouth.edu/pages/faculty/ken.french/data_library.html) defines the source portfolios; the study uses the captured vintage, not a refreshed download.

October 2007 is eligible in the common calendar and is the original expanding model's **first-ranked** match. Thus the claim that eligibility structurally prevents the review from selecting the original leading episodes is too broad. A 1946 origin and late-2021 origins are excluded here; 2007 is not. Differences persist under common eligibility because the models use different information and distance definitions. This does not establish that one model has “replicated” or invalidated the other.

Both expanding specifications have lower five-year medians than the common unconditional distribution, yet both have positive medians and only two negative five-year cases. The one- and three-year medians differ markedly. The controlled result supports treating elevated valuation as a reason to examine long-horizon outcomes; it does not establish an exact expected return, a crash date, or a statistically identified causal valuation effect. No new significance or forecast-calibration claim follows from these eight selected cases. The prior review's valuation-omission and random-reference sensitivities remain relevant and were not rerun or replaced here.

Sector conclusions also depend on the comparison. However, the corresponding paired mean is +15.35 versus **−1.00**; a positive median alone does not establish a reliable energy allocation. ## 3. The original joint rich-and-calm condition

The completed test is the original conjunction **CAPE percentile ≥90 AND Baa–Aaa percentile ≤20**, not an energy condition and not the separate tightening-onset study. It preserves each source series and its reference history. Only the percentile convention changes: original full-history average ranks versus strictly prior expanding ranks with at least 120 observations. Both versions use identical eligible origins, horizons and return definitions.

The full-history rule reproduces the original **26** selected months exactly before applying common maturity. Removing the immature November and December 2021 origins leaves **24**. The expanding rule admits **74** qualifying months on the same July 1964–July 2021 calendar. This change is not an outcome-selected threshold adjustment.

| Monthly pool, 684-month common calendar | Qualifying months | One-year real market median | Three-year median | Five-year median | Five-year losses |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Original full-history ranks | 24 | 15.72% | 21.06% | −11.20% | 16/24 (66.67%) |
| Strictly prior expanding ranks | 74 | 11.26% | 23.84% | 10.19% | 28/74 (37.84%) |
| Unconditional reference | 684 | 9.92% | 24.28% | 42.88% | 161/684 (23.54%) |

These monthly observations overlap extensively. Chronologically retaining the earliest qualifying origin and then waiting at least 60 months gives **two** full-history cases, February 1997 and May 2021, versus **six** expanding cases, July 1964, March 1993, March 1998, January 2005, February 2014 and May 2021. Their five-year medians are **+39.63% and +31.46%**, with **0/2 and 2/6 losses** respectively. These tiny sets and an arbitrary first qualifying month cannot provide a calibrated probability; their purpose is to expose how much monthly weighting changes the apparent result. Zero losses among two cases is not proof of safety.

Two additional checks constrain interpretation. Extending both eligible calendars to January 1947 increases the common baseline to 894 months and the expanding pool to 88, while the original full-history pool remains 24. The expanded pool's five-year median is +18.13%; chronological 60-month thinning still yields six cases but moves the first to May 1963 and its median to +50.04%. Changing the full-history tie convention alone from average ranks to a strict empirical CDF produces 27 qualifying months and a −8.93% median. Thus window and tie conventions matter, but ties alone do not explain the expanding pool's sign change. Horizon-specific 12- and 36-month thinning is also retained as a sensitivity in the output.

The strong negative five-year joint-pool headline is **not robust** to replacing hindsight ranks or reducing overlapping observations. Some rank specifications still look weaker than their same-calendar unconditional reference, so this result does not show that high valuation is harmless. It does rule out presenting the original monthly loss fraction as a stable forward probability. It supplies no independently validated joint alarm and no basis for multiplying component indicator probabilities.

## 4. What the profit-share/CAPE calculation actually changes

The captured quarterly profit share is **12.0711599859%**; the 1947–1989 mean is **6.0946301439%**. At unchanged GDP, moving from the former to the latter changes aggregate profits by **−49.5108162681%**. This arithmetic is correct. It does not demonstrate that the historical average is a future equilibrium.

| Precisely defined scenario | CAPE proxy | Already-dated proxy months changed |
|---|---:|---:|
| Original August construction | 42.00070823 | 0 |
| Replace all post-splice earnings by GDP × pre-1990 mean share × fixed splice scale | 50.29776577 | 38 |
| Scale only the latest August earnings observation by the share ratio, as a timing illustration | 42.22530433 | 1 |
| Review 002: September price/CPI assumptions, pre-2000 median share, all post-splice months replaced | 49.65907450 | 39 |

The 50.30 construction changes **July 2023 through August 2026**, leaving **82 of the trailing 120 denominator months unchanged**. It rewrites 38 already-dated proxy earnings observations, each using its corresponding interpolated GDP and the fixed historical share. The approximately 49.51% change concerns the current aggregate-profit level; it is not a uniform 49.51% reduction applied to all 38 historical months. The baseline also retains the original CPI splice/carry-forward convention for exact replication; that is separate from the no-imputation rule used for return outcomes above.

The one-month illustration is not a future forecast either: it edits August history. It demonstrates why the position and number of affected months matter. An actual forward CAPE scenario must preserve the existing denominator and specify future nominal GDP, profit-share transition and duration, the mapping from aggregate NIPA profits to index per-share earnings, future inflation, and the price path. Each future monthly observation enters the rolling denominator while an older one exits. Neither 50.3 nor a doubling of CAPE follows mechanically from a prospective halving of current profits. S&P earnings coverage, share counts, foreign earnings and buybacks further limit the NIPA mapping.

The prior profit forecast check also reproduces. A pre-2000 fitted level mean-reversion model has one-, three- and five-year test RMSEs of **1.587, 3.326 and 4.175 percentage points**, compared with persistence **0.822, 1.395 and 1.579**, across **102, 94 and 86 overlapping post-2000 origins**. This is evidence against the tested fixed historical equilibrium model's forecast usefulness, not evidence that profit shares never revert. The earlier within-era mean-reversion associations are compatible with a changing equilibrium.

## 5. Research disposition

The common-window comparison and joint-pool rerun are complete and reproducible. They do not require postponing the research synthesis or inventing a new forecast. The remaining limits are substantive: revised histories, model choice, target selection, small numbers of economic episodes, proxy earnings and missing actual investment implementations. Settling a claim about forecasting or allocation would require a registered future rule, dated available inputs, a defined investment universe, an implementable decision policy and prospective outcomes. None is supplied by retroactively choosing the most favorable historical specification.
