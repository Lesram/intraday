## Trailing stop mechanics (why it's giving back so much)

From `adaptive_exits.py`:
- Trailing activates after 3× ATR favorable move (~$3-6 on typical positions)
- Trail distance is 3.0× ATR in chop (line 156)
- Once trailing activates, it ratchets: `trailing_stop = max(trailing_stop, highest_favorable - trail_distance)`

The problem: in chop, price goes up 3× ATR (trailing activates), then oscillates. The trailing stop is 3× ATR from the HIGH, which is 6× ATR from the entry. But in chop, a 3× ATR reversal from the high is normal noise. The trailing stop is set at a distance that's reasonable for trending markets but too tight for chop oscillation.

**This is the same class of problem as pyramid_cut**: exit thresholds calibrated for trending conditions being applied in chop.

## Proposed trailing-stop experiment (Exp4)

**Hypothesis**: In chop regime, the trailing stop distance (3.0× ATR from high) is too tight. Normal chop oscillation routinely retraces 3× ATR from highs, triggering the trailing stop on trades that would have recovered.

**Proposed change**: In chop, widen the trailing stop from 3.0× ATR to 5.0× ATR. This gives trades room to breathe through normal chop oscillation while still protecting against genuine reversals.

**Alternative**: Disable trailing stops entirely in chop and rely solely on timeout/max_hold as the exit (since timeout is 100% win rate). This is more aggressive but has the strongest data support.

**Expected value**: If the NVDA trade had a 5× ATR trail instead of 3×, it would have survived the reversal and captured most of the $13.81 MFE. Even capturing 50% of the 2 trailing-stop trades' MFE = +$9.34 per session.

**Evidence needed**: 2-3 more sessions of Exp1A data to confirm the trailing-stop giveback pattern persists. If it does, Exp4 is the highest-value next experiment after Exp2.
